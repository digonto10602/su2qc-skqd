| C3' | add: every shortest-depth circuit must show its reference string at least 3 sigma above the accidental-acceptance expectation (the bit-order test); criterion 3 is kept, computed and reported, but labelled as not a device test | **APPROVED** | 2026-09-30 | The energy criterion was meant to catch a bit-order error, but the sector fills from accidentally-valid noise at every planned budget, so the energy reproduces whether the ordering is right or not and whether the processor works at all: it is the decoder's exhaustive property, established by gate E2 over all 4096 strings. A bit-order error instead shows as clean shots landing on the wrong strings, and the circuit's output is concentrated (0.883/0.889 at the shortest depth), so the reference-string count distinguishes the cases. It has already demonstrated this on real data: 6 hits against 2.02 expected from noise over the hardware runs, P = 0.017. Honest limitation recorded: at the clean fractions this device delivers the test is statistically weak, and reaching 3 sigma per circuit depends on D3''-H0's sizing; on a poor patch it could fail for want of signal rather than for a wiring error, which would be a true statement about the device. The old criterion is relabelled rather than deleted so the record shows that the energy did reproduce and why that carries no information about the hardware. |
| H0P-Y' | gate H0P's factor-of-three ratio band becomes a bracket: analytic bound <= scheduled simulation <= unscheduled simulation | **APPROVED** | 2026-09-30 | The three disagree by understood factors on the same snapshot, circuit and coherence times: unscheduled simulation 0.191 (no delays at all), scheduled simulation 0.0336 (penalty e^-1.74), analytic formula 0.0028 (penalty e^-3.79) -- the formula is 11.8x more pessimistic than the scheduled simulation. That is deliberate conservatism, quantified: it charges every error as fatal, but 22.4 per cent of its phase-error budget (0.576 of 2.568 units) sits in windows before a qubit's first rotation or after its last, where a phase error is provably invisible, one qubit's trailing window alone accounting for 0.397. Each quantity keeps the job it suits: the bound is parameter-free with a closed form, so the shot planner and the patch selector use it; the scheduled simulation is the honest prediction. Requiring the ordering tests mutual consistency instead of asserting a number none of them claims. This also removes the failure mode that produced the assistant's misreported '1.13x agreement', which compared a bound against a measurement as though it were a prediction. Includes the one-flag fix for the unscheduled path's missing delays, which is why the dry run reported the wrong hypothesis as confirmed. |
