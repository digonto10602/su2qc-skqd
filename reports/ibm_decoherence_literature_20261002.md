## 0. Vocabulary (defined once)

- **Clean fraction $f$**: the probability that one shot carries no error at all; measured in this project with the *reference-string statistic* (the count of the circuit's most probable ideal string, corrected for accidental hits, divided by shots $\times$ $p_{\rm ref}$ $\times$ the readout factor $0.82$; `skqd.skqd.reference_string_test`).
- **Idle window**: an interval in which a qubit waits (an explicit `delay` in an ALAP-scheduled circuit) while other qubits are gated.  ALAP = "as late as possible" scheduling, which keeps each qubit in $|0\rangle$ as long as it can.
- **$T_1$, $T_2^{\rm echo}$, $T_2^*$**: energy-relaxation time; dephasing time measured *with* one refocusing pulse (Hahn echo, the number in IBM's calibration record); dephasing time of *free* evolution with no pulse (what an idle qubit in a circuit actually experiences).  Low-frequency ("quasi-static") noise shortens $T_2^*$ far below $T_2^{\rm echo}$.
- **Dynamical decoupling (DD)**: a sequence of $\pi$ pulses ($X$, $Y$) inserted into an idle window that multiplies to the identity and reverses the sign of slowly varying phase errors, so that they cancel over the window.  It cancels nothing that varies faster than the pulse spacing, and every pulse costs its own gate error.
- **ZZ crosstalk**: an always-on interaction $\zeta\, Z_i Z_j/4$ between neighbouring transmons that shifts qubit $i$'s frequency by $\pm\zeta/2$ depending on the state of qubit $j$.  It is a *coherent* phase error, not a decay.
- **Pauli twirling / randomized compiling**: random Pauli gates inserted around each two-qubit gate and undone afterwards; it converts coherent errors into random Pauli errors without changing the average error per gate.
- **Staggered / context-aware DD**: DD in which neighbouring qubits receive *different* (mutually orthogonal) pulse patterns so that the ZZ phase between them is also reversed; identical simultaneous pulses on both partners leave ZZ untouched.
- **Nats**: natural-log error units; a budget of $S$ nats multiplies $f$ by $e^{-S}$.

Two consequences **(planner arithmetic)**:

1. The gap between the echo-end prediction and the measurement is a factor $0.2114/0.0413 = 5.1$.  In error units it is $\ln 5.1 = 1.63$ nats of *extra* dephasing beyond what the echo $T_2$ accounts for; the PTA sum says the free-induction windows carry $8.67 - 1.01 = 7.7$ nats more than the echo windows, which over-explains the gap (the PTA bound charges every phase error as fatal; `H0P-Y'` in `data/H0_replan_owner_decisions.md` records this 11.8x conservatism).  Either way the *dominant* loss is quasi-static phase accumulated in idle windows, which is exactly the error class DD is built for.
2. A readout-corrected $P_0$ below $1/2$ in a Ramsey experiment needs a coherent phase $|\phi| > \pi/2$ over the window, i.e. an effective detuning $|\delta|/2\pi > 1/(4T)$: **5.7 kHz** over the 44.03 $\mu$s window, **11.4 kHz** over the 22.02 $\mu$s window.  Pure decay cannot do this.  The phase comes from a frequency offset of the qubit relative to its drive frame, from ZZ with neighbours that were also put into superposition by the same pub (every one of the 12 qubits was), or both.

**Can $\zeta$ or $\delta$ be fitted from the pilot's Ramsey data?**  No.  Per qubit the data are two numbers ($P_0$ at $T$ and at $T/2$) and the model has at least two unknowns already for a single detuning, $P_0 = \tfrac12\big(1 + e^{-T/T_2^*}\cos\delta T\big)$, with the cosine aliased ($\delta T$ and $2\pi - \delta T$ are indistinguishable); with ZZ to $m$ superposed neighbours the signal is $\prod_j \cos(\zeta_j T/2)$ times the decay, adding $m$ unknowns.  The design (all 12 qubits in superposition at once, no phase sweep, no neighbour-state control) cannot separate the three.  A dedicated ZZ test needs, per edge, a Ramsey on one qubit with the neighbour in $|0\rangle$ and in $|1\rangle$ and a phase sweep of the final pulse — about $21$ edges $\times$ 2 states $\times$ several phases, i.e. a job of its own.  The planner's call: do not spend QPU seconds on characterising the mechanism; the decision statistic is the circuit's own $f$, and a circuit-level A/B test answers "does staggered DD help" directly.

## 2. Why XY4 + twirling did not help on fez — what the sources and the data support

**(a) The fez factorial had no statistical power on $f$.**  The cells' reference hits are 2 / 1 / 1 / 0 (`H0_diag.json`); the accepted counts 35 / 27 / 25 / 17 are dominated by near-clean strings (the M4.4 near-miss term, `data/H0_replan_owner_decisions.md`).  J2/J1 $= 0.77$ with a relative Poisson $\sigma$ of $\sqrt{1/27 + 1/35} = 0.26$ **(planner arithmetic)**, so "DD hurt" was never established; the owner approved D8' "on the parameter-free argument and NOT on the factorial's ranking" and left the door open: *"Client-side multi-cycle decoupling on the long-idle qubits, with runtime decoupling off, may return as a preregistered comparison once the pilot shows a clean fraction above about 1 per cent."*  The pilot shows 4.1 %.

**(b) Twirling cannot raise a clean fraction; it can only cost pulses.**  Randomized compiling converts coherent errors into stochastic Pauli errors while keeping the *average* error per gate the same (Wallman & Emerson, arXiv:1512.01098, "tailors the noise into stochastic Pauli errors ... while introducing little or no experimental overhead"; the worst-case error drops, the average fidelity does not rise).  A clean shot needs *no* error in the sequence; its probability is governed by the average fidelity of the layers, which twirling preserves.  So for a sampling-based method the expected gain from twirling on $f$ is zero, and the `active-accum` strategy adds single-qubit gates to every active qubit in every twirled two-qubit layer (412 such layers in the ALAP circuit, `validation/S2D_levers.json: data.rows.L1_seed_alap.compile.twoq_layers`) wherever they cannot be merged.  Twirling earns its place when one *rescales* an expectation value (catalogue D11–D14); it has no place in a clean-fraction run.

**(c) The server-side XY4 on fez applied the same sequence on every idle qubit at the same times.**  The runtime's `dynamical_decoupling` options expose `sequence_type` (XX, XpXm, XY4), slack placement, scheduling method and `skip_reset_qubits` — no coupling-map staggering (verified in the installed `qiskit_ibm_runtime 0.49.0` `DynamicalDecouplingOptions`).  Identical simultaneous $\pi$ pulses on both partners of a ZZ-coupled pair flip both $Z$'s and leave $Z_iZ_j$ invariant: the ZZ phase is *not* refocused.  This is the central point of Tripathi, Chen, Khezri, Yip, Levenson-Falk, Lidar, "Suppression of crosstalk in superconducting qubits using dynamical decoupling", arXiv:2108.04530 (Phys. Rev. Applied 18, 024068, 2022; IBM cloud devices): ZZ crosstalk is suppressed only when the pulses on the coupled pair are staggered, and "the XY4 dynamical decoupling sequence loses its universality if the drive frequency is not much larger than the system-bath coupling strength".  Niu, Todri-Sanial, Bronn, "Multi-qubit dynamical decoupling for enhanced crosstalk suppression", arXiv:2403.05391 (Quantum Sci. Technol. 2024; IBM processors) report a staggered protocol improving circuit fidelity by **19.7 %** (static crosstalk) and **8.5 %** (driven crosstalk) relative to the plain X2 sequence.  Seif, Liao, Tripathi, Krsulich, Malekakhlagh, Amico, Jurcevic, Javadi-Abhari, "Suppressing correlated noise in quantum computers via context-aware compiling", arXiv:2403.06852 (2024; IBM hardware, 10-qubit layer) report **+18.5 % layer fidelity** from mutually orthogonal (Walsh–Hadamard) $X$-sequences on adjacent qubits.  Coote, Dimov, Maity, Hartnett, Biercuk, Baum, "Resource-efficient context-aware dynamical decoupling embedding for arbitrary large-scale quantum algorithms", arXiv:2409.05962 (PRX Quantum 6, 010332, 2025; 127-qubit IBM devices) describe GraphDD, which refocuses "both quasi-static single-qubit dephasing and crosstalk idling errors" exactly over arbitrary idle-delay layouts with a minimal number of $X$ gates and report "orders of magnitude" fidelity gains over Qiskit's standard embedding.  Evert et al., "Syncopated dynamical decoupling for suppressing crosstalk in quantum circuits", arXiv:2403.07836 (2024; Rigetti transmons) reach the same conclusion on a different vendor's chip.

**(d) The pulse cost is real and was never subtracted.**  The runtime pass pads every idle gap longer than twice the sequence; on the fez canary that is 232 windows and 0.213 nats, a factor $e^{-0.213} = 0.81$ on $f$ before any benefit (`S2D_levers.json: data.dd_ceiling`).  J2/J1 $= 0.77 \pm 0.20$ is consistent with "XY4 did nothing beyond its pulse cost" **(planner arithmetic)** — and equally consistent with a modest gain hidden under the pulse cost.  The fez data cannot tell.

**Does a staggered / crosstalk-aware variant target the kingston signature?**  Yes for both candidate mechanisms: any $\pi$-pulse train reverses a *static* frequency offset (the detuning reading of the $P_0 < 1/2$ signature); only a *staggered* train reverses the ZZ reading.  Simultaneous XY4 covers the first and not the second; the Walsh–Hadamard context-aware embedding covers both between idle neighbours.  What no DD covers is dephasing faster than the pulse spacing and ZZ with a neighbour that is being *gated* during the window (the spectator case; `ContextAwareDynamicalDecoupling` handles ECR/CX spectators by construction, and on a CZ-based Heron it still guarantees orthogonal sequences on adjacent idle qubits).

## 3. The candidates, with sources

Each entry: what it is, the source (fetched abstract page), the reported effect and hardware, and what it means for this circuit.

**C1. Context-aware (staggered, Walsh–Hadamard $X$) DD inserted client-side into the explicit idle windows.**  Seif et al., arXiv:2403.06852 (IBM, +18.5 % layer fidelity); implementation: `qiskit.transpiler.passes.ContextAwareDynamicalDecoupling` in the installed qiskit 2.5.2 (its docstring cites exactly this paper).  Same family: Niu et al. arXiv:2403.05391 (+19.7 % / +8.5 %), Tripathi et al. arXiv:2108.04530, GraphDD arXiv:2409.05962.  Uses only $X$ gates, which are in kingston's basis.  **Prototype on the committed pilot circuit** (section 6): with `min_duration = 128 dt` (512 ns) it inserts 862 pulses at a record pulse cost of 0.203 nats (factor 0.82); with the pass's default it inserts 3066 pulses (0.733 nats, factor 0.48 — too costly); with `min_duration = 250 dt` (1 $\mu$s) 370 pulses, 0.089 nats (0.92).  The statevector is identical to the base circuit's (max $|\Delta\psi| = 0$) and the scheduled duration is unchanged (46.42 $\mu$s).  Does not touch the signed family (the unitary is unchanged; only identities are inserted into delays).

**C2. XY4 inserted client-side into every window $\ge 2\times$ the sequence length.**  Viola & Lloyd, arXiv:quant-ph/9803057 (Phys. Rev. A 58, 2733, 1998; the bang-bang principle); the sequence survey of Ezzell, Pokharel, Tewala, Quiroz, Lidar, "Dynamical decoupling for superconducting qubits: a performance survey", arXiv:2207.03670 (Phys. Rev. Applied 2023; 60 sequences on three IBM devices): UR and QDD families best overall, but "CPMG and XY4 achieved comparable performance when pulse intervals were optimized".  Pokharel & Lidar, "Demonstration of algorithmic quantum speedup", arXiv:2207.07647 (PRL 2023): the speedup on ibmq_montreal appears only with DD.  Prototype: 194 of the 338 windows qualify (they hold 94.5 % of the 337 $\mu$s of idle time), 776 pulses, 0.173 nats (factor 0.84); statevector identical; duration unchanged.  Needs $Y$, which is not in the basis: $Y = R_z(\pi/2)\,X\,R_z(-\pi/2)$ up to a global phase, so the sequence is translated to `rz`/`x` after insertion (zero-duration `rz`).  Does not refocus ZZ between two simultaneously idle neighbours.

**C3. XY4 only in long windows (the "selective DD" idea).**  Das, Tannu, Dangwal, Qureshi, "ADAPT: Mitigating idling errors in qubits via adaptive dynamical decoupling", arXiv:2109.05309 (MICRO 2021): applying DD only where it helps gives up to 5.73x over no DD and 1.2x over universal DD, using decoy circuits with known outcomes to decide.  Prototype: windows $\ge 1\ \mu$s are 61 of 338 but hold 74.6 % of the idle time; XY4 there costs 244 pulses, 0.057 nats (factor 0.94); windows $\ge 2\ \mu$s: 30 windows, 61.7 % of the idle, 120 pulses, 0.029 nats.  The physics argument for this cell: the benefit of DD scales with the window length (quasi-static phase grows linearly with $T$, its square with $T^2$) while the pulse cost is per window, so the short windows are the ones where DD is most likely a net loss.

**C4. Robust sequences: UR$_n$ and KDD.**  Genov, Schraft, Vitanov, Halfmann, "Arbitrarily accurate pulse sequences for robust dynamical decoupling", arXiv:1609.09416 (PRL 118, 133202, 2017): the UR family compensates pulse imperfections and dephasing to arbitrary order, pulse number linear in the order.  Souza, Álvarez, Suter, "Robust dynamical decoupling", arXiv:1110.6334 (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 370, 4748, 2012): the KDD composite-pulse sequence (five phased $\pi$ pulses per inversion).  Both need phased pulses ($R_z$ frame changes around $X$, free on IBM) and more pulses per window than XY4 (KDD: 20 per cycle), so their pulse cost on this circuit would be several times C2's; their advantage is robustness to pulse errors, which at $1.2\times10^{-4}$ per $X$ on this patch is not the binding constraint.  Deferred: not in this round.

**C5. Learned DD (GADD).**  Tong, Zhang, Pokharel, "Empirical learning of dynamical decoupling on quantum processors", arXiv:2403.02294 (PRX Quantum 6, 030319, 2025; IBM, up to 100 qubits): genetic search over DD strategies "significantly outperforming canonical sequences".  Needs many hardware evaluations of the objective — not affordable inside 571 s.  Not now.

**C6. Pauli twirling.**  Wallman & Emerson, arXiv:1512.01098.  Cannot raise $f$ (section 2b); costs pulses.  Ranked last; not tested.

**C7. ZZ-aware qubit mapping / crosstalk-aware scheduling.**  Murali et al., "Software mitigation of crosstalk on noisy intermediate-scale quantum computers", arXiv:2001.02826 (ASPLOS 2020; three 20-qubit IBM devices, "up to 5.6x" error-rate improvement by serialising crosstalk-prone pairs); Ketterer & Wellens, "Characterizing crosstalk of superconducting transmon processors", arXiv:2303.14103 (Phys. Scheduling: ALAP with explicit delays is already adopted (prompts/23, lever 0.710x duration); the two scheduling papers above would be the next lever but need a scheduler the project does not have.

**C8. Readout mitigation.**  Nation, Kang, Sundaresan, Gambetta, "Scalable mitigation of measurement errors on quantum computers" (M3), arXiv:2108.12518 (PRX Quantum 2, 040326, 2021); van den Berg, Minev, Temme, "Model-free readout-error mitigation for quantum expectation values" (TREX), arXiv:2012.09738. **C9. ## 5. What a positive result would and would not mean

- If C1 or C2 beats the baseline by the preregistered factor, the clean fraction moves and the D3' plan shrinks as $1/f$; whether it reaches the signed $0.1$ is a separate, preregistered read on the adopted cell's lower 95 % bound.
- A ratio equal to the Aer null (0.82 / 0.84 / 0.94) means the idle noise is white on the 0.3–20 $\mu$s scale and DD cannot help this circuit; the result then closes the DD question for 2x2 on this device class with a number, which is itself worth the 23 s.
- A ratio *below* the null would mean the pulses cost more than the record says (or ZZ with gated neighbours is made worse) — a finding, to be reported, not averaged.
