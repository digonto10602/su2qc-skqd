Jargon, defined once.  **Clean-shot fraction f** = the probability that one shot of a circuit suffers
no error at all (manual Step 4.4; `src/skqd/device_req.py`):
$f = (1-\epsilon_2)^{n_{2q}} (1-\epsilon_1)^{n_{1q}} (1-\epsilon_{ro})^{n_{meas}} \, e^{-S_{\rm idle}}$,
with $\epsilon_2, \epsilon_1, \epsilon_{ro}$ the two-qubit, one-qubit and readout error rates, $n$ the
gate counts and $S_{\rm idle}$ the idle/memory budget in nats.  **Signed budget** (amendment 01 item 2):
mean $f \ge 0.1$ over the 44-circuit family and worst $f \ge 0.05$.  **QCCD** = quantum charge-coupled
device: trapped ions are physically moved between gate zones, so any pair can interact (all-to-all)
at the price of transport time.  **Memory error per depth-1 circuit time** = Quantinuum's name for
the error a qubit accumulates while the register is transported for one layer of two-qubit gates on
all qubits.  **HQC** = Hardware Quantum Credit, Quantinuum's billing unit.  **eHQC** = the same unit
for Quantinuum-hosted emulators.  **QCUP** = the Quantum Computing User Program of Oak Ridge
(OLCF), a proposal-based access route.  **Virtual Rz** = a Z rotation absorbed into the phases of
later pulses, carrying no error.  **D3'** = the project's shot rule (`scripts/h0_support_plan.py`).

## 3. Evaluation of 2x3 on each candidate (planner arithmetic; `scratch/planner/quantinuum_2x3_prototype_20261002.json`)

Method.  Gate-only $f$ with `skqd.device_req.clean_shot_fraction(n_2q, n_1q, 20, eps2, eps1, eps_ro)`
at $n_{2q} = 2158$ (all-to-all devices), $n_{1q} = 3053$ (virtual Rz; Quantinuum and IonQ both make
Rz virtual), $n_{meas} = 20$; for lattice devices $n_{2q} = 2158 \times$ overhead with the measured
heavy-hex overhead 2.53 (`validation/S2.json`) for IBM and an **ESTIMATE** 1.8 for square lattices
(not computed; the exact value needs a routing run, which the executor does only if the owner asks).
SPAM is the mean of Quantinuum's 0/1 values.

Memory (idle) term, Quantinuum only, **ESTIMATE**.  The signed circuit is nearly serial: its
two-qubit critical path is 1925 gates deep (prototype), so a QCCD device executes about 1925 rounds of
"transport, cool, gate" per shot regardless of how many zones it has (the 4-zone gate-count bound is
only 540 rounds).  Per-round time scenarios: low 0.5 ms (70 us gate + ~300 us post-shift cooling +
short transport, Helios paper Sec. II.3), mid 1.1 ms (the Helios 55 ms depth-1 layer divided by its
49 gates), high 4.4 ms (55 ms divided by the 12.25 rounds of 4 parallel gates a 98-qubit layer needs,
i.e. a full sort per round).  Memory rate per qubit per second: Helios $5\times10^{-4}/55\,$ms
$= 9.1\times10^{-3}$/s; H2-1 from the emulator's linear dephasing rate 0.0028 /s; H2-2 and H1-1 scaled
by their performance-validation memory errors (1.2 / 2.0 and 2.2 / 2.0).  $S_{\rm idle} = 20 \times
1925 \times t_{\rm round} \times \text{rate}$.  **The Quantinuum emulators (H2-2E, Helios-1E) contain
the real transport schedule and noise model; their $f$ replaces this estimate (prompts/26 Stage E).**

Reading the table.

- **Only Quantinuum's QCCD machines meet the signed error bar on published measured numbers.**  H2-2
  and Helios-1 both have $\epsilon_2 \approx 8\times10^{-4}$, $\epsilon_1 \approx 3\times10^{-5}$,
  SPAM $\lesssim 10^{-3}$: gate-only $f = 0.150$ and $0.164$, 1.5-1.6x above the mean bar.  H1-1
  (20 qubits exactly, $\epsilon_2 = 9.7\times10^{-4}$) is marginal at 0.111; H2-1 fails the mean bar.
- The decisive unknown is the **memory (transport) term**, because the circuit is 1925 two-qubit
  gates deep.  H2-2's published memory error ($1.2\times10^{-4}$ per depth-1 time, the lowest of the
  fleet) keeps $f$ above 0.11 in every scenario; Helios' memory error is 4x larger and
  leakage-dominated, so Helios drops below 0.05 in the pessimistic scenario.  **Rank: H2-2 first,
  Helios-1 second, both to be settled by their emulators**, which carry the real transport schedule
  (H2 emulator data sheet: "dephasing noise due to transport and qubit idling", computed from "the
  duration the qubit spends in transport or while idling").
- Every other platform fails by 2 to 13 orders of magnitude on gate errors alone; the neutral-atom
  and superconducting numbers are vendor-published medians read on the pages cited, and no routing
  or idle term was needed to reach the verdict.  IonQ Tempo would reach the worst-case bar only.
- **Cost is the second obstacle.**  Quantinuum bills $\mathrm{HQC} = 5 + C\,(N_{1q} + 10 N_{2q} + 5 N_m)/5000$
  per job (Azure provider page; Helios costing page: "Rz operations excluded", PhasedX and ZZ counted;
  $N_m$ counts the 20 initialisations and 20 measurements).  For one 2x3 shot: $(3053 + 21{,}580 +
  200)/5000 = 4.97$ HQC.  The full D3-type campaign at $f \approx 0.14$ is $\sim 10^5$ shots, i.e.
  **about 0.5 million HQC**.  The only published rate is the Azure Standard plan, USD 125,000 per
  month for 10,000 HQC (12.5 USD/HQC), which would price the campaign at 6-8 million USD; the
  pay-as-you-go rate is not public.  A commercial purchase is therefore not realistic; the realistic
  routes are a **QCUP allocation** (free to approved projects; "Requests for machine credits must be
  justified using results from an emulator"; monthly allocations, request by the 25th of the preceding
  month; default hardware quota 0 HQC, emulator quota "6000 seconds") or a **research agreement with
  Quantinuum** (Sales@Quantinuum.com; national programmes such as the Danish DeiC/SDU Helios access
  exist).  Even a device pilot of the two $k = 1$ circuits at 1000 shots each costs about 10,000 HQC
  (one full Azure Standard month).
- Machine time: at the mid scenario (2.1 s per shot) the campaign is 60-75 hours of QCCD time,
  spread over the 10,000-shot-per-job limit (11 jobs per sector); the queue and the commercial /
  development calendar ("Every month, a calendar is sent to Quantinuum users") set the wall-clock.

**Plain answer.**  Today, the Quantinuum System Model H2-2 (and Helios-1) can run the signed 2x3
family within the signed error budget on published numbers -- gate-only $f = 0.15$-$0.16$ against the
0.1 bar, with the transport/memory term estimated to cost 5-30 % (H2-2) or 15-80 % (Helios) of that
and to be measured on the vendor emulator before any hardware request.  No other accessible device
comes within two orders of magnitude.  What it would take: an emulator run (free under QCUP, eHQC
otherwise) to fix $f$; then an HQC allocation of order $5\times10^{5}$ HQC for the full campaign, or
$\sim10^{4}$ HQC for a two-circuit device pilot that measures $f$ on hardware.

## 5. What is NOT established

- No Quantinuum number here was measured by this project; all are vendor RB-sweep values of
  unspecified date (the performance-validation page carries no date; raw data in
  https://github.com/CQCL/quantinuum-hardware-specifications).
- The memory term is an ESTIMATE in three scenarios spanning a factor 4 in $f$ for Helios; the
  emulator settles it.  The emulator itself is a vendor model ("System Model H2 error models will
  mature over time", emulator data sheet).
- The HQC-to-USD rate is the Azure Standard-plan equivalent; the pay-as-you-go and research rates are
  not public.
- Square-lattice routing overhead 1.8 is an uncomputed ESTIMATE used only for devices that fail by
  orders of magnitude anyway.
- QCUP eligibility: the OLCF page lists "US national labs, universities, government, and industry";
  quantinuum.com says "Researchers in the United States may apply".  Whether the owner qualifies is
  the owner's question to OLCF.
