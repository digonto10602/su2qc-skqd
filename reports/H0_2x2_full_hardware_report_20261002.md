## 0. Vocabulary (each term defined once, in plain words)

- **SU(2) lattice gauge theory with staggered quarks**: the model of the project document.  Quarks live on the sites of a 2-row ladder, gauge fields on the links; the gauge field on each link is truncated to spin $j_\ell \in \{0, \tfrac12\}$.  The $2\times2$ ladder has 4 sites and 4 links, 82 physical states, split into sectors by **baryon number** $B$ (a conserved quark count): 38 states at $B=0$, 20 at $B=\pm1$, 2 at $B=\pm2$.
- **Gauss's law**: the constraint that the colour charge flowing into a site balances the charge on it; only states obeying it at every site are physical.  The **decoder** is the classical routine that reads a 12-bit measurement string, checks Gauss's law and link consistency, and either returns a physical state or rejects the string.  A **codeword** is the 12-bit string assigned to one physical state.
- **SKQD (sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalization)**: the quantum processor prepares states that spread a reference configuration over the physical configurations that matter, measures them in the configuration basis, and the classical side diagonalizes the exact Hamiltonian in the span of the accepted configurations.  The quantum device only chooses the subspace; the energy is variational (never below the true ground energy) for any subspace.
- **Coarse step of order $k$**: one circuit that applies each Hamiltonian term's exact time evolution for a time $k\,\Delta t$ in sequence, with $\Delta t = \pi/W$ and $W$ the sector's spectral width.  The project's circuits are 7 **reference configurations** (5 at $B=0$: the Dirac sea and the four one-meson configurations; 2 at $B=1$: the diquark on either even site) times $k=1,2,3,4$: 28 circuits.
- **Ritz value $E_R$**: the lowest eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian restricted to the chosen subspace.  **Residual $r_H = \|(H - E_R)\psi_R\|$**: how far the subspace eigenvector is from being a true eigenvector.  **Weinstein interval**: $[E_R - r_H, E_R]$, which rigorously contains some exact eigenvalue; it is the ground energy's interval only if the nearest exact level is the ground state (**gap assumption**).  **Kato-Temple interval**: the tighter $[E_R - r_H^2/(\alpha - E_R), E_R]$ with $\alpha$ a lower bound on the first excited level (here the second Ritz value, so also gap-assumed).
- **Clean fraction $f$**: the probability that one shot of a circuit carries no error at all.  Measured here with the **reference-string statistic**: the count of the circuit's most probable ideal output string, minus the accidental hits expected from uniform noise, divided by shots $\times$ the ideal probability of that string $\times$ the readout factor 0.82.  The **mixture estimator** is a second statistic (one parameter separating the ideal distribution from uniform noise over all accepted strings); both are reported.
- **Garbage acceptance $a$**: the fraction of uniformly random 12-bit strings the decoder accepts into a sector.  At $2\times2$ it is exactly the sector dimension over $2^{12}$: $a_{B=0} = 38/4096 = 0.00928$, $a_{B=1} = 20/4096 = 0.00488$ (gate E2, exhaustive).  **Near-clean string**: a string with a few errors that still passes every decoder check; it is accepted but carries no physics.
- **$B_{\rm all}$ / $B_{\rm sig}$** (project labels, defined in `prompts/24` decision P9): $B_{\rm all}$ is every accepted state of the sector; $B_{\rm sig}$ ("signal support") keeps only the states whose count exceeds the uniform-noise expectation $\mu_s = N a/{\rm dim}$ at the one-sided $3\sigma$ Poisson level.  $B_{\rm sig}$ removes uniform noise, not near-clean false positives.
- **Noise saturation (rule C22)**: once $N a / {\rm dim} \gtrsim 5$, every sector state is hit about five or more times by accidentally-valid noise alone, so $B_{\rm all}$ is the whole sector whether or not the processor worked.  At $2\times2$, $N a/{\rm dim} = N/4096$ **(planner arithmetic, exact because $a = {\rm dim}/4096$)**.
- **$T_1$, $T_2^{\rm echo}$, $T_2^*$**: energy-relaxation time; dephasing time measured with one refocusing pulse (the number in IBM's calibration record); dephasing time of free evolution (what an idle qubit in a circuit experiences).  **Idle window**: an interval in which a qubit waits while others are gated.  **ALAP**: "as late as possible" scheduling, with the waits written as explicit `delay` instructions.
- **Dynamical decoupling (DD)**: $\pi$-pulses inserted into idle windows that cancel slowly varying phase errors; each pulse costs its own gate error.  **XY4**: the four-pulse sequence $X\,Y\,X\,Y$.  **Context-aware (staggered) DD**: neighbouring qubits get mutually orthogonal pulse patterns so that the always-on $ZZ$ interaction between them is also cancelled.  **Pauli twirling**: random Pauli gates around each two-qubit gate, undone afterwards; it reshapes errors but does not reduce the average error, so it cannot raise $f$.
- **Preregistration**: writing the circuits, shot counts, decision rule and prediction to a committed file *before* the device data exist.  **Calibration fingerprint (rule D9)**: a hash of the calibration-record content the prediction actually reads; submission is allowed only when the live hash equals the preregistered one, and the hash is recorded again at retrieval.
- **Patch**: the 12 physical qubits chosen for the run; **rule R1'**: exhaustive search over all embeddings of the circuit's interaction graph on the full device, scored by the idle-aware clean fraction.
- **Measurement gate**: a gate whose PASS means "preregistered, measured, verified, consistent", never "the number is good".  Gates `H0_kpilot`, `H0_ddtest`, `H0_2x2`, `S2D_levers` are of this kind.
- **CIPSI**: a classical selected-configuration-interaction method that grows a subspace by scoring Hamiltonian neighbours of the current subspace.  It is the manual's main classical control.

## 5. What has been learnt

Each lesson is tied to the file that carries it.

**Physics and method**

1. **At $2\times2$ the SKQD energy is not a device measurement.** With $a = {\rm dim}/4096$ the sector fills from accidentally-valid noise once $N \gtrsim 5 \times 4096$ shots **(planner arithmetic)**; the run used 69505 and 64402.  Garbage-only input reproduces $E_0$ in 100 of 100 seeds (`validation/H0_2x2.json` -> `data.baselines.*.garbage_only`).  The manual anticipated this qualitatively (Step 0: "any $10^3$-shot run saturates its sectors"); the project quantified it (rule C22) and built the controls.  The same warning appears in the 2026 literature on sample-based diagonalization (section 6).
2. **The certification machinery works as specified.** Weinstein and Kato-Temple intervals computed from the exact residual contain $E_0$ in every case, and at $B=1$ the residual $5.95\times10^{-3}$ resolves the near-degenerate cluster (gap $0.0418$), which the manual listed as a stretch goal (`validation/H0_2x2.json` -> `data.energies.B=1.B_sig`).
3. **Accepted shots are not clean shots.** The decoder accepts near-clean strings at a rate comparable to pure noise; the yield inversion of manual Step 4.4 overstated the clean fraction fifteen-fold on fez (0.0101 vs $6.65\times10^{-4}$; `validation/H0_model.json` -> `data.C2_pooled_device_clean_count`).  The reference-string statistic, cross-checked by the mixture estimator, is the right measurement, and the manual's yield model needed the near-clean term (rule M4.4).  This was derivable from gate E2's exhaustive enumeration on the day the manual was written.
4. **A signal support can be defined without an exact reference.** $B_{\rm sig}$ (counts above the $3\sigma$ Poisson noise expectation) is computable on any lattice from $N$, $a$ and dim; at 2x3 and beyond it is the natural object to compare with CIPSI at equal size.

**Device**

5. **Idle-time dephasing, not gate count, is the loss that matters on Heron for this circuit family.** The gate-only model predicted $f = 0.2195$ on fez; the device gave $6.65\times10^{-4}$ clean (`validation/H0_canary.json` -> `data.f_comparison`; `validation/H0_model.json`).  The circuit runs 43.7 $\mu$s at 0.233 qubit-time utilisation (amendment item 3), and the in-circuit $T_2^*/T_2^{\rm echo}$ ratio is 0.17 on fez and 0.10 on kingston (`validation/S2D_idle.json` -> `data.t2_bracket.fallback_ratio`; `validation/H0_kpilot.json` -> `data.decision.r_eff`).  Only the scheduled simulation at the measured $T_2^*$ post-dicts the device (1.05x; the echo end is 49.6x off).
6. **Runtime XY4 plus twirling did not help on fez; client-side XY4 restricted to long windows helped 2.8x on kingston; staggered context-aware DD collapsed.** Fez factorial J2/J1 $= 0.77$ with a Poisson relative error of 0.26 **(planner arithmetic)**, i.e. no information; kingston same-job ratios T2 2.79 $[2.47, 3.16]$, T3 2.82 $[2.50, 3.19]$, T1 0.012 $[0.004, 0.031]$ (`validation/H0_ddtest.json` -> `data.decision.cells`).  Twirling cannot raise a clean fraction (it preserves the average error) and only costs pulses; it has no place in a sampling run.
7. **Patch selection and scheduling are first-order levers.** The idle-aware rule R1' was worth 1.41x on the record that flew and 3.31x device-wide on fez (amendment item 1, `data/H0_patch_select.json`); ALAP with explicit delays cut the scheduled duration to 0.710x (`validation/S2D_levers.json` -> `data.verdict.duration_ratio_best`).  Neither alone reaches the bar; together with DD on the kingston patch the $k=1$ circuits reached $f \approx 0.11$-$0.13$.
8. **The calibration-content fingerprint was stable through the day.** `84d59cbf9b5973d1` at preregistration, submission and retrieval of both the DD test and the full run (`validation/H0_2x2.json` -> `data.live.R1`); on fez it had fired on the one real recalibration (`CLAUDE.md` status).  Readout on the kingston patch: min diagonal 0.9417 against the live expectation 0.9525; survival 0.834 against the design 0.82.
9. **The depth ladder at 2x2 is uninformative as run.** 94 % of the shots sit at $k=4$, so the $k$-resolved growth of $B_{\rm sig}$ mostly tracks shots; the clean fraction of the $k=2$-$4$ circuits is not measurable with the present estimators at their low $p_{\rm ref}$ (C6 ruling).

**Process**

10. **Measurement gates plus preregistration kept the record honest at every turn.** Every hardware step has a committed prediction and decision rule before the data, a fingerprint-guarded submission, a dry run, and a PASS that means "measured" rather than "good"; two NO-GO verdicts (canary, pilot) and one FAIL (diag) are in the record with their numbers.  The one exception is a positive one: the full run proceeded under an explicit, scoped owner waiver of the signed bar (`data/owner_decision_20261002_run_below_signed_budget.md`), not under a quietly relaxed criterion.
11. **The whole 2x2 campaign cost 102 s of QPU time**, 53 s for the full run, against the 600 s open-plan allowance; the estimate-to-billed ratio of the full run was 41.7 s estimated to 53.0 s billed **(planner arithmetic: 1.27)**.
12. **Simulator dry runs cannot predict DD gains** (Aer's relaxation on a delay is Markovian), so the dry-run ratios 0.71-0.79 vs nulls 0.82-0.94 (commit `c5e308d`) were path checks only, as `prompts/24` P10 said in advance.

## 6. Is it publishable yet?

### 6.1 Where the field stands (abstracts fetched 2026-10-02)

*SKQD and SQD.*  The method's source is Yu, Robledo Moreno et al., arXiv:2501.09702 (Heron processor with the Frontier supercomputer; impurity models up to 41 bath sites; convergence proven "under the working assumptions of Krylov quantum diagonalization and sparseness of the ground state").  Rosanowski, Eisinger, Funcke, Poschinger, Schmidt-Kaler, arXiv:2510.26951, applied SKQD to the Schwinger model with a $\theta$-term on trapped-ion and superconducting processors and report the phase structure and an effective-Hilbert-space reduction.  Robledo-Moreno et al., arXiv:2405.05068, is the chemistry demonstration on up to 77 qubits with configuration recovery.

*Non-Abelian gauge theory with matter on hardware.*  Atas, Zhang, Lewis, Jahanpour, Haase, Muschik, arXiv:2102.08920 (2021) realised "for the first time a non-Abelian gauge theory with both gauge and matter fields on a quantum computer" (SU(2), IBM, variational eigensolver, meson and baryon masses).  Ilčić, Majumdar, Mathew, Ali, Earnest-Noble, Raychowdhury, arXiv:2602.18080 (2026) report SU(2) hadron dynamics on a 60-site (1+1)D lattice on a 156-qubit IBM processor with the loop-string-hadron encoding.  So "first SU(2) with dynamical matter on hardware" is not available as a claim; the project's own manual claims only "first quantum-centric spectroscopy of a non-Abelian gauge theory with dynamical matter in two spatial dimensions ... on 2x3" (Step 0), and that is the 2x3 result, not the 2x2 one.

*The random-baseline critiques.*  van Rossum, Cohn, Shrapnel, Gupta, arXiv:2608.11569: "classical uniform random sampling can reproduce SQD benchmarks as noise increases the diversity of sampled configurations ... any fair benchmarking protocol of SQD must explicitly control diagonalization size over unique samples".  Vaquero-Sabater et al., arXiv:2605.23697: "recovery alone (starting from randomly generated configurations) can efficiently construct accurate CI spaces".  Gaberle and Jattana, arXiv:2605.02494: the number of configurations needed for fixed accuracy "grows exponentially with the system size" for Heisenberg and Hubbard models even with optimal selection.  Bonilla Vargas, arXiv:2608.05314 (review): on whether the quantum sampler beats classical selected CI, "the negative verdict ... concerns reproducible, same-active-space comparisons on molecular electronic structure".  Bhuiyan, Chu, Lou, Zheng, arXiv:2607.18196: SQD on Heron is robust to layout and mitigation choices after a few recovery iterations, and "very large budgets slightly worsen recovered energies".  Park, Kang, Ahn, Jeong, arXiv:2607.10227: engineered code-space constraints as recovery signals (Ising up to 36 sites).

*Dynamical decoupling on IBM Heron.*  Seif et al., arXiv:2403.06852: context-aware DD, +18.5 % layer fidelity on a 10-qubit layer.  Hickman, Wu, Quiroz, arXiv:2506.18010: $ZZ$-robust DD gives "at least a 3x improvement in the fidelity decay rate" on fixed-coupler IBM devices, while on **tunable-coupler devices "ZZ-robust sequences perform nearly equivalent to non-robust DD"** — which is consistent with T2 and T3 agreeing on kingston (a tunable-coupler Heron) and makes the T1 collapse more surprising, not less.  Tenev, Nedev, Vitanov, arXiv:2604.23228: Grover on three Heron generations with topological DD.  Ezzell et al., arXiv:2207.03670: the sequence survey.

### 6.2 What the 2x2 result can claim

- **A validated, preregistered, end-to-end SKQD pipeline for a 2+1D SU(2) gauge theory with staggered quarks on a Heron processor**: intertwiner-labelled basis, Gauss-law decoder (0 mismatches on 58 hardware strings; random acceptance equal to the exhaustive value), exact structured coarse-step circuits (exact to $2.6\times10^{-14}$), ALAP scheduling with client-side DD, a calibration-content guard, and certified Ritz energies with their controls.  This is what the manual asked of 2x2 (Step 9.1) and it is delivered.
- **A measured clean fraction on 12-qubit, 588-CZ, 44-$\mu$s circuits**: $0.1271$ $[0.1084, 0.1479]$ with DD, $0.0413$ $[0.0361, 0.0470]$ without, on one patch of ibm_kingston on one day; and the same-job DD ratios of section 3.
- **A signal support of 35 of 38 and 19 of 20 states** above the $3\sigma$ noise line, whose Ritz energies lie within $5.5\times10^{-4}$ and $5.7\times10^{-6}$ of the exact values with certified intervals, and which sit at the 5th and 28.5th percentiles of random equal-size bases.
- **A quantified statement of why the $B_{\rm all}$ energies carry no device information** ($N a/{\rm dim} \approx 16$; garbage-only reproduces $E_0$), which is the fair-benchmarking point of arXiv:2608.11569 made on a gauge theory.

### 6.3 What it cannot claim

- **Not an SKQD accuracy result.** The manual says so (Step 0, Step 9.1); the saturation analysis proves it; $E_R(B_{\rm all}) = E_0$ is a property of the decoder (gate E2), not of the device.
- **Not a device-support-quality result.** Recall 1.0 is reached by noise alone; $B_{\rm sig}$ removes uniform noise but not near-clean false positives (rule M4.4); and the one classical control that matters at equal size (CIPSI) was not run on the hardware data because at 35 of 38 states it is trivially near-exact.
- **Not a claim about the circuit family's clean fraction.** The signed bar was read on $k=1$ circuits only; the $k=2$-$4$ circuits' $f$ is unmeasured (C6 ruling).
- **Not a replicated DD result.** One job, one patch, one calibration content; the context-aware cell's collapse is unexplained; arXiv:2506.18010 would predict little difference between staggered and plain DD on a tunable-coupler device, so the T1 number needs a mechanism test before it is reported as a finding rather than an anomaly.
- **Not neural-enhanced.** No learned model touched the hardware data (section 2, rows 7.1-7.5).
- **Not any physics number beyond reproduction of known exact values**: $M_B$, $\Delta_0$, $V(r)$ are defined by the manual on 2x3.

### 6.4 Verdict

**Publishable as:** (a) a methods/technical note on *running sample-based diagonalization honestly at a size where noise saturates the subspace* — the clean-fraction estimators, the near-clean term, the saturation rule, the signal-support definition and the garbage/random baselines, with the 2x2 hardware run as the worked example; and (b) possibly, after replication, a short note on *client-side XY4 in long idle windows raising the clean fraction 2.8x on a Heron r2 with the context-aware variant failing*, which would be of interest to practitioners because the vendor's runtime DD gave no gain on the same circuit class.  Both would be modest contributions and neither is a gauge-theory physics paper.

**Not publishable as:** an SKQD result for SU(2) lattice gauge theory, a device-support-quality measurement, a demonstration of the manual's workflow validation (which it defines on 2x3), or a neural-enhanced method.  Submitting the 2x2 energies as evidence that "the device finds the ground state" would be exactly the error arXiv:2608.11569 and arXiv:2605.23697 warn against, and a referee who reads the garbage-only baseline would say so.

**What a publishable physics claim needs (in the order the plan already fixes):**
1. A lattice where noise cannot saturate the sector: at 2x3 the $B=0$ sector has 677 states and the whole-space random acceptance is 0.146 %, so the saturation parameter stays well below 1 at the planned shot counts **(planner arithmetic, e.g. $2\times10^5 \times 0.00146/677 = 0.43$, an upper bound since the per-sector acceptance is smaller)**; this needs the 2x3 device question (amendment item 4) answered with declared gate durations and $T_1/T_2$, because the 2x3 circuit is longer and more serial than the 2x2 one and the idle budget dominates.
2. The primary endpoint of Step 6.3: device vs CIPSI at equal $|B|$ with bootstrap bands over circuits, on that lattice, with $B_{\rm sig}$ as the device support.
3. The clean fraction measured on the whole circuit family ($k = 2$-$4$), which needs either an estimator calibrated at low $p_{\rm ref}$ or a different statistic (e.g. the mixture estimator validated at those depths on the simulator).
4. Repeat runs: a second calibration window on the same patch, a second patch, ideally a second device, so that the DD gain and the clean fraction carry uncertainty bands that include day-to-day drift.
5. The ML step credited or nulled under Step 7.5 on hardware data, if the title is to keep the word "neural".
6. The release bundle of Step 10: raw bitstrings, calibration records, circuits, decoder.

## 7. Open problems and next steps

**Open problems**
- *The context-aware DD collapse* (section 3).  Three one-cell tests of about 4 s each would separate the hypotheses: context-aware at `min_duration` 1 $\mu$s (370 pulses, prototype in the literature report), an $XX$-only staggered variant, and the T1 circuit with the pulses on the two worst-$T_2^*$ qubits removed.
- *The coherent-phase signature in the pilot's Ramsey data* (9 of 12 qubits with $P_0 < \tfrac12 - 3\sigma$; `validation/H0_kpilot.json` -> `data.decision.ramsey_detuning_signature_information`).  Its mechanism (frequency offset vs $ZZ$) is unmeasured; a dedicated per-edge Ramsey with the neighbour in $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ is a job of its own.
- *The family-level clean fraction* (C6).  Without it, the signed bar is a statement about 7 of 28 circuits.
- *The 2x3 device* (amendment item 4) and *the shot quota* (item 5), both owner decisions.  Superconducting 2x3 is ruled out by the idle budget; the trapped-ion assessment cannot be made without declared durations and coherence times.
- *The S3 gate for 2x3* (Aer device-model recall $\ge 0.9$) was never evaluated; the GPU throughput calibration exists (`validation/S3.json`, 0.0199 s per shot at 20 qubits per `CLAUDE.md`).
