## 1. What was asked

Compare the subspaces that SKQD built from sampled bit strings with subspaces chosen by a purely classical method, at the same subspace size, on every ladder SKQD was run on: $2\times2$, $2\times3$ and $2\times4$, coupling $g^2=4$, mass $m=3g^2/16$, sectors $B=0$ and $B=1$ (twice the baryon number $2B=0,2$).  Energies are in lattice units ($a=1$).

**Words used.**  A *basis state* is one gauge-invariant configuration of the sector (one bit string that SKQD can measure).  *Selected configuration interaction* (selected CI) keeps a chosen set $S$ of basis states, diagonalises the Hamiltonian $H$ inside the span of $S$, and grows $S$ by states judged important. The lowest eigenvalue of $H$ restricted to $S$ is the *variational energy* $E_{\rm var}(S)$; it is never below the true ground energy $E_0$.  *CIPSI* (configuration interaction by perturbation with multiconfigurational zeroth-order wave functions selected by iterative process; B. Huron, J.-P. Malrieu and P. Rancurel, J. Chem. Phys. 58, 5745 (1973), doi:10.1063/1.1679199) is the selection rule used here: every basis state $a\notin S$ that $H$ connects to $S$ gets the second-order perturbation-theory (*PT2*, Epstein-Nesbet form) importance

$$e_a=\frac{|\langle a|H|\psi_S\rangle|^2}{E_{\rm var}(S)-H_{aa}},$$

where $\psi_S$ is the lowest eigenvector in $S$ and $H_{aa}$ the diagonal matrix element; the states with the largest $|e_a|$ are added and the step repeats.  The sum $E_{\rm PT2}=\sum_a e_a$ estimates the energy still missing, so $E_{\rm var}+E_{\rm PT2}$ is an improved estimate of $E_0$ (an estimate, not a bound).  $W(S)=\sum_{b\in S}|\langle b|\Omega\rangle|^2$ is the exact ground-state weight captured by $S$ ($|\Omega\rangle$ the exact ground state).  The *certificate width* is the length of the interval that provably (under the gap condition of the gates) contains $E_0$: for $B=0$ the Kato-Temple width $r_H^2/(E_1-E_R)$ with the exact first excited energy $E_1$, for $B=1$ the Weinstein width $r_H$, where $r_H=\lVert(H-E_R)\psi_R\rVert$ is the residual of the Ritz vector (the lowest eigenvector in the subspace, energy $E_R$) with the exact $H$.

**Two reference curves.**  *Oracle*: the $|S|$ basis states with the largest exact ground-state weight. It maximises $W$ at fixed size (it is not guaranteed to minimise the energy) and it needs the exact answer. *Random*: 200 random subsets of the sector of the same size that always contain the SKQD reference states (the package's CV4 baseline, seeds 23 to 222).

## 2. What was done

* CIPSI starts from the SKQD reference states (`skqd.krylov.references`) and grows by a factor 1.25 per iteration (target size $\lceil 1.25\,|S|\rceil$) until $E_{\rm var}-E_0\le10^{-10}$ or the whole sector.  Two other schedules are recorded at every comparison point: doubling (factor 2), and a fixed batch of 8 ($2\times2$, $2\times3$) or 16 ($2\times4$) states per iteration, the setting of the package's existing `skqd.controls.cipsi` in gate S1.  Code: `src/skqd/selected_ci.py`; driver `scripts/selected_ci_baseline.py`; tests `tests/test_selected_ci.py`.

* The CIPSI set of exactly $n$ states is the last iterate with at most $n$ states plus the top of its ranking, i.e. a prefix of the next CIPSI step, so CIPSI sets are nested in $n$ and $E_{\rm var}$ falls monotonically with $n$.  CIPSI, oracle and random sets are evaluated with `gate_CV.Sector.basis_metrics` and `skqd.skqd.certify`, the code path of the SKQD gates.

* "Size to reach SKQD": the smallest CIPSI (main schedule) or oracle subspace whose $E_{\rm var}-E_0$ is at or below SKQD's own $E_R-E_0$ at that row (bisection on the nested family).

* SKQD rows: $2\times2$ = the ibm_kingston hardware session (`validation/H0_2x2.json`, significance-filtered basis $B_{\rm sig}$ and the full accepted basis $B_{\rm all}$); $2\times3$ = noiseless Aer sampling of the frozen circuits (`validation/C1_IDEAL.json`, the paper's Table of noiseless SKQD) and of the native circuits (`validation/C3_AER.json`) at the $f=0.10$ plan and at $2\times10^5$ shots, plus the noisy proxy emulation of the plan (`validation/CV_2x3_plan.json`, three seeds at each of $f=0.05,0.10,0.15$); $2\times4$ = `validation/SKQD_2x4.json` (plan and $2\times10^5$ points).

* Runs: 2x2 `python scripts/selected_ci_baseline.py --lattice 2` 3 s wall; 2x3 `python scripts/selected_ci_baseline.py --lattice 3` 61 s wall; 2x4 `python scripts/selected_ci_baseline.py --lattice 4` 139 s wall, BLAS 5 threads under `taskset -c 0-4 nice -n 10` on the laptop (i7-8750H).

## 4. What the result means

* **Count.**  There are 32 equal-size rows in which SKQD is not exact to round-off (all lattices, sectors and SKQD runs in the JSON files).  CIPSI with the main schedule has a lower variational energy than SKQD in 31 of them; the best of the three CIPSI schedules in 32; the oracle in 32.  SKQD is below the median random subset in 32 and below all 200 random subsets in 30.
* **Exception: 2x4 B=1, SKQD_2x4 plan ($|B|=1768$).**  SKQD's error $1.28\times10^{-3}$ is below CIPSI's $2.57\times10^{-3}$ with the main schedule (CIPSI needs 2063 states to match it), but the fixed-batch schedule gives $2.97\times10^{-4}$ and the oracle $3.00\times10^{-4}$. SKQD captures $1-W$ = $1.7\times10^{-4}$ against CIPSI's $1.3\times10^{-3}$.
* **Size for equal accuracy.**  The CIPSI subspace (main schedule) that reaches SKQD's error has between 0.46 and 1.17 times SKQD's number of states (median 0.73) over these 32 rows.
* **2x2 $B=1$ (gap $E_1-E_0$ = $4.184\times10^{-2}$).**  The overlap of the main-path CIPSI vector with the exact first excited state, $|\langle 1|\psi_S\rangle|^2$, exceeds 0.01 at 7 of 10 iterates (sizes 2 to 12; largest 0.499 at $|S|=5$, where the overlap with the ground state is 0.491).  In that range the selection follows a mixture of the two nearly degenerate levels (see the schedule dependence below).
* **2x3 $B=1$ (gap $E_1-E_0$ = $2.436\times10^{-2}$).**  The overlap of the main-path CIPSI vector with the exact first excited state, $|\langle 1|\psi_S\rangle|^2$, exceeds 0.01 at 17 of 22 iterates (sizes 3 to 148; largest 0.498 at $|S|=38$, where the overlap with the ground state is 0.487).  In that range the selection follows a mixture of the two nearly degenerate levels (see the schedule dependence below).
* **2x4 $B=1$ (gap $E_1-E_0$ = $6.035\times10^{-3}$).**  The overlap of the main-path CIPSI vector with the exact first excited state, $|\langle 1|\psi_S\rangle|^2$, exceeds 0.01 at 28 of 35 iterates (sizes 4 to 2169; largest 0.499 at $|S|=290$, where the overlap with the ground state is 0.488).  In that range the selection follows a mixture of the two nearly degenerate levels (see the schedule dependence below).
* SKQD rows not below all 200 random subsets: 2x2 B=0 H0_2x2 ibm_kingston hardware, B_sig ($|B|=35$, 5.0 % of the random subsets at or below SKQD); 2x2 B=1 H0_2x2 ibm_kingston hardware, B_sig ($|B|=19$, 28.5 % of the random subsets at or below SKQD).
* **Schedule dependence.**  The ratio between the worst and the best of the three CIPSI schedules at an SKQD size reaches 2.9 in the B=0 rows (median 1.00), 296.0 in the B=1 rows (median 16.79).
* **Plain reading.**  At every lattice size reached here, a classical selection that may apply $H$ to its current vector finds a lower-energy subspace than SKQD's sampled one at the same size in 31 of 32 rows (main schedule; 32 of 32 with the best schedule), and reaches SKQD's accuracy with a median 0.73 times SKQD's number of states.  SKQD's subspaces are far better than random subsets of the same size.  This is the expected outcome in sectors small enough to enumerate, and the paper should say so plainly: the comparison measures how close quantum sampling comes to a good classical selection; it is not evidence of an advantage over it, and it says nothing about the regime SKQD is designed for.

## 5. Problems and assumptions

* At these sizes (at most 12843 basis states per sector) the whole sector can be diagonalised exactly in seconds, so this comparison is about the *quality of the subspace*, not about cost.  CIPSI needs the matrix elements of $H$ to every connected state and runs entirely classically; SKQD's point is to obtain the subspace from quantum samples when the sector is too large to enumerate.  Nothing here tests that regime, and no statement about quantum advantage or disadvantage at scale follows from it.

* CIPSI depends on its growth schedule and, in sectors with symmetry-equivalent or zero-importance states, on how ties are broken.  Both effects are reported (three schedules in Table 1; tie counts and the gate S1 cross-check in Table 3) rather than tuned away.  A state-averaged selection over the lowest few roots, the usual remedy for near-degenerate levels such as the $B=1$ cluster, was not tried.

* The oracle maximises captured weight, not energy, so CIPSI can match or beat it in energy at equal size. Both CIPSI and the oracle use information SKQD does not have (the exact $H$ applied to the current vector, or the exact ground state).

* $E_{\rm PT2}$ is an estimate, not a bound; $E_{\rm var}+E_{\rm PT2}-E_0$ can be negative.

* The $2\times2$ SKQD subspaces come from a hardware session whose noise saturated the sector (`validation/H0_2x2.json`, labelled "not a device test (saturation)"); only $B_{\rm sig}$ is a non-trivial $2\times2$ comparison.  The SKQD rows of $2\times3$ and $2\times4$ are emulations (noiseless Aer sampling, or the S1 noise proxy), not hardware.

* The $2\times4$ exact ground states come from `scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh` at tolerance $10^{-10}$ without a fixed start vector (`skqd.exact.sector_reference`), so $E_0$, the weights and $E_{\rm tol}$ there are reproducible only to about that tolerance (`validation/SKQD_2x4.json` itself records a $B=0$ $E_{\rm tol}$ difference of $4.6\times10^{-9}$ between its two evaluations).  This is far below every energy difference compared here.

* The ranking rounds $|e_a|/\max|e_a|$ to $10^{-10}$ before breaking ties by index, so the CIPSI sets do not depend on the BLAS thread count (Table 3, re-run at one thread).
