## 1. What was asked

Build Qiskit Aer noise models for IBM, IonQ and Quantinuum devices from internet-published numbers: depolarizing error per gate, thermal relaxation over gate durations, readout error, idle/memory error where published; all-to-all connectivity for IonQ and Quantinuum, a published coupling map (or the documented Heron topology) for IBM; transpile to each device's native gates; document every choice with its equation.

## 2. Modelling choices and equations

All channels are *Pauli channels* (a random Pauli operator $X$, $Y$ or $Z$ is applied with some probability). Then every noisy shot is a pure state with a few Pauli 'faults' inserted (a *trajectory*), and the probability that a shot has no fault at all — the *clean-shot fraction* $f$ — is an exact product.

**(a) Gate error.**  Vendors publish the *average gate infidelity* $r$ from randomized benchmarking (RB). A uniform depolarizing channel with that $r$ applies a non-identity Pauli with total probability equal to the *process infidelity*: $$p_{\rm fault}=r\,\frac{d+1}{d},\qquad p_{\rm fault,1q}=\tfrac32 r_{1q},\quad p_{\rm fault,2q}=\tfrac54 r_{2q},$$ with $d=2^{n_q}$, each of the $d^2-1$ non-identity Paulis with probability $p_{\rm fault}/(d^2-1)$, applied right after every native gate.  (The package's `device_req.clean_shot_fraction` uses $r$ itself as the fault probability; that number is also reported, as `f_pkg`.)

**(b) Angle dependence of the Quantinuum RZZ gate.**  Only the vendor's emulator publishes it: $$r_{2q}(\theta)=\Big(a\,\frac{\tilde\theta}{\pi}+b\Big)\,r_{2q},\qquad a=1.518,\ b=0.241,$$ normalised so that the fully entangling $\tilde\theta=\pi/2$ gives $r_{2q}$.  Here $\tilde\theta=|\theta \bmod^{\pm}\pi|\in[0,\pi/2]$, because $\mathrm{RZZ}(\pi-x)=(Z\otimes Z)\,\mathrm{RZZ}(-x)$ up to free virtual $R_z$ gates.  IonQ and IBM: no published angle dependence, constant $r_{2q}$.

**(c) Quantinuum memory (idling and transport) error.**  Published as a memory error $p_{\rm mem}$ per qubit per *depth-1 layer* (all qubits re-paired and gated once, for a maximally dense circuit).  Our circuits are not dense; I charge them the number of dense layers with the same two-qubit-gate count, $$D_{\rm eq}=\frac{N_{2q}}{\lfloor n_{\rm active}/2\rfloor},$$ i.e. after every $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ two-qubit gates a $Z$ error with probability $p_{\rm mem}$ on every active qubit (memory noise is dephasing in the vendor's emulator).  This is my own label and estimate, not a vendor quantity; the variants `*_nomem` switch it off so its weight is visible.  Helios: $p_{\rm mem}$ = linear + quadratic parameter at one depth-1 transport per layer.

**(d) IBM and IonQ idle error (thermal relaxation).**  Each qubit idles between its gates (IBM: as-soon-as-possible schedule with the published gate durations, gates in parallel; IonQ: serial execution, one gate at a time, since no parallelism is published).  Over an idle time $\tau$ the amplitude-damping + dephasing channel with $T_1,T_2$ is replaced by its Pauli-twirl approximation $$p_X=p_Y=\frac{1-e^{-\tau/T_1}}{4},\qquad p_Z=\frac{1-e^{-\tau/T_2}}{2}-\frac{1-e^{-\tau/T_1}}{4}.$$  During a gate the RB error already contains decoherence, so only idle time is charged.  Variants `ionq_*_noidle` switch idling off (this is what IonQ's own published simulator noise model does: depolarizing after each gate only).

**(e) Readout.**  Classical bit flips $P(1|0)$, $P(0|1)$ on every measured bit, applied to the sampled string. The clean fraction uses the mean $\bar\varepsilon_{\rm ro}=\tfrac12[P(1|0)+P(0|1)]$: $$f=\prod_{\rm channels}(1-p_{\rm fault})\;(1-\bar\varepsilon_{\rm ro})^{n}.$$

**(f) Native gates and compilation.**
- Quantinuum: $\{R_z\ (\text{virtual, free}),\ \mathrm{PhasedX}=R(\theta,\phi),\ \mathrm{RZZ}(\theta)\}$; IonQ Forte: $\{\text{virtual }R_z,\ \mathrm{GPi},\ \mathrm{GPi2},\ \mathrm{ZZ}(\theta)\}$, where an arbitrary $R(\theta,\phi)$ costs 2 pulses unless $\theta\in\{\pi,\pi/2\}$.  Compilation: qiskit `transpile` to $\{rz,rx,ry,rzz\}$ (optimization level 0, seed 7), then every run of one-qubit gates merged into one $R(\theta,\phi)$ plus one virtual $R_z$ (exact: $U=e^{i\gamma}R_z(\phi+\lambda)R(\theta,-\lambda)$ from the ZXZ Euler angles).  Verified: native vs logical output probabilities agree to 6.5e-14 on the frozen 2x3 circuit B0_ref117_k1 (`data/checks/native_compile_check.json`).
- IBM Heron: $\{cz, rz, sx, x\}$ (IBM docs, fractional gates off), routed by qiskit Sabre layout and routing at optimization level 1, seed 7, on a 156-qubit heavy-hex coupling map *reconstructed* from the documented structure (8 rows of 16 qubits joined by 7 x 4 bridge qubits: 156 qubits, 176 couplers, matching IBM's '156 qubits, heavy-hexagonal lattice' and '176 two-qubit couplings').  The real bridge positions are not public without the API; the routing cost of a reconstructed heavy-hex lattice is the quantity of interest.  Verified: routed vs logical probabilities agree to 6.5e-13 on the same circuit (21 physical qubits used).

**(g) Two placements of the same model.**  `analytic_f` counts faults on the native circuit.  The trajectory engine (`src/traj_sparse.py`) works on the circuit's IR gate list, because the package's sparse simulator keeps the state small there (frozen 2x3 B0_ref117_k1: at most 998 strings in IR form, 0.7 s, against 442368 strings and 201 s in native form; a 28-qubit dense trajectory costs minutes).  The mapping: one native RZZ per IR two-qubit gate (cx, cz at $\pi/2$; cp($\lambda$) at $|\lambda|/2$; rzz($\theta$) at $\theta$), its depolarizing channel right after the IR gate; one PhasedX per maximal non-diagonal run of IR one-qubit gates (a cx contributes a Hadamard on its target on each side).  A uniform depolarizing channel commutes with every unitary on its own qubits, so moving it across the local one-qubit gates that distinguish the IR gate from the native one changes nothing.  The frozen 2x3 IR (`CircuitFactory.coarse_step`) has more two-qubit gates than its optimised native form, so the trajectory engine charges the frozen family more than `analytic_f` does; both are reported.

## 4. What the models mean

- For Quantinuum the model is gate-dominated plus a memory term whose size for a sparse 20–28-qubit circuit is not published; `*_nomem` brackets it from below.
- For IonQ, a serial schedule of 0.9 ms two-qubit gates against $T_2$ = 1 s makes idle dephasing the largest error source of any circuit with more than a few hundred gates; whether IonQ's hardware suppresses it (dynamical decoupling) is not published, so `ionq_*_noidle` is the optimistic bound.
- For IBM, routing on heavy-hex multiplies the two-qubit count: 2158 RZZ -> 8573 CZ on the frozen 2x3 circuit B0_ref117_k1; 6131 CZ (class A) and 2281 CZ (class B) on 2x4 B0_ref102_k1 (`data/checks/native_compile_check.json`); with the published CZ errors no clean shots remain (report 003).

## 5. Problems and assumptions

- Idle and memory noise are modelled as Pauli channels (Pauli-twirl approximation); coherent dephasing, leakage, crosstalk and SPAM-leakage are not modelled.
- Paper calibration snapshots (IBM) are single days; the paper does not say median or mean.  The public IBM platform table of 2026-10-21 (best and layered 2q error, median readout) is kept in the IBM JSON as a cross-check.
- The Quantinuum $D_{\rm eq}$ memory charge and the IonQ serial schedule are my estimates; both are switched off in the variants.
- The heavy-hex map is a reconstruction (same size, degree and coupler count as Heron), not the device's map.
