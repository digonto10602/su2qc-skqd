## 1. What was asked

Sample every circuit of the compressed families (and the frozen 2x2 / 2x3 circuits for comparison) under the published Quantinuum device models -- primary `quantinuum_h2_2` (H2-2), secondary `quantinuum_helios_1` (Helios-1) -- with enough shots for a meaningful SKQD analysis inside about 24 h of laptop time; decode with the package codec; run the full SKQD analysis (basis $B_\mathrm{all}$, Ritz energy $E_R-E_0$, recall of $S_{99.9}$, ground-state weight $W$, certificate, CV1-CV5, convergence with shots) on the noisy samples and on ideal samples of the same size; report acceptance, $f_\mathrm{hit}$ and $r_\mathrm{nc}$.

Terms used below (all defined in the package, none invented here):

- *trajectory / shot*: one simulated run; Pauli faults drawn from the device model are inserted into the exact circuit, the final state is computed exactly (sparse simulator) and one bit string is drawn and passed through readout flips.
- *acceptance*: fraction of shots the package decoder accepts as a valid basis state of the circuit's sector.
- $f_\mathrm{hit}$: (shots equal to the reference string) / (shots $\times$ ideal probability of that string); an estimate of the fraction of shots that behave as noiseless.
- $r_\mathrm{nc}$: $f_\mathrm{hit}/f_\mathrm{ideal}$ with $f_\mathrm{ideal}$ = readout survival of the reference $\times$ fraction of trajectories whose pre-readout sector distribution is within total-variation distance $10^{-3}$ of the ideal one; $r_\mathrm{nc}\approx 1$ means the reference-hit estimator measures the near-clean fraction without bias.
- $B_\mathrm{all}$: references plus every accepted state; $E_R$: lowest eigenvalue of $H$ projected on it.
- certificate: an interval that must contain the exact $E_0$ -- Kato-Temple with the exact first excited energy $E_1$ at $B=0$, Weinstein ($E_R \pm r_H$, $r_H=\lVert H\psi_R - E_R\psi_R\rVert$) at $B=1$; *width* = its length.
- $S_{99.9}$: smallest set of basis states holding 99.9 % of the exact ground state's weight; *recall* = fraction of it inside $B_\mathrm{all}$; $W$ = exact ground-state weight inside $B_\mathrm{all}$.
- CV1-CV5: the convergence criteria of gate CV (`scripts/gate_CV.py`): CV1 $E_R(N/2)-E_R(N)\le E_\mathrm{tol}$; CV2 certificate width at $N/2$ and $N$ below 0.1 (B=0) / 0.15 (B=1) with $E_0$ inside; CV3 $E_R(k\le4)-E_R(k\le5)\le E_\mathrm{tol}$ at equal shots $N_\mathrm{eq}$ per circuit; CV4 every prefix beats the 2.5th percentile of 200 random equal-size bases by at least $E_\mathrm{tol}$; CV5 $W\ge0.99$.  P = pass, F = fail.

## 2. What was done

1. Timing probes and the budget: `noise/notes/campaign_budget.md`.  Per circuit $N_c' = \lceil N_c/f_c\rceil_{100}$ from the noiseless plan of record $N_c$ and the model clean fraction $f_c$; cuts listed there and in section 4.
2. Sampling: `scripts/skqd_sample.py` (one chunk = one job $<$ 30 min) through `scripts/skqd_queue.py`, sparse Pauli-trajectory engine `noise/src/traj_sparse.py` (validated against Aer to $2.7\times10^{-12}$, report 003), seeds crc32(lattice|family|device|circuit|skqd|chunk).  Every chunk records its clean-state deviation from the exact target (max over the campaign below).
3. Analysis: `scripts/skqd_analyse.py`, the package's own machinery (`skqd.campaign33.analysis`, `scripts/gate_CV.py`); the ideal control draws the same number of shots per circuit from the exact target distribution (no readout error).  Bootstrap: shots resampled within each circuit (B resamples per cell in the table).
4. Aggregate: `scripts/skqd_aggregate.py` -> `noise/data/results/noisy_skqd_campaign_summary.json`; loader `results.load(lattice, family, device, "noisy_skqd")`.

### 3.5 Where noise changes a CV verdict

| lattice | family | device | sector | criterion | noisy / ideal | sector dimension | $\lvert B_\mathrm{all}\rvert$ noisy / ideal | CV4 margin / $E_\mathrm{tol}$ noisy / ideal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2x3 | frozen | H2-2 | B=1 | CV4 | **F** / P | 426 | 397 / 360 | 0.198 / 1.407 |

CV4 asks the device basis to beat random bases *of the same size* by at least $E_\mathrm{tol}$.  Noise-born states enlarge $B_\mathrm{all}$ towards the whole sector, where random bases of that size are nearly as good; the margin then shrinks below $E_\mathrm{tol}$ although the energy, the recall and the certificate are fine.  This is the one place where the noise, rather than the shot count, changes a verdict.


## 4. What the results mean

- Certificates: $E_0$ lies inside the certificate in 30 of 30 (cell, sector) rows with noisy samples and in 30 of 30 with ideal samples.  Lowest recall of $S_{99.9}$ with noise: 0.964.
- CV pass counts over 30 rows, noisy (ideal): CV1 30 (30), CV2 26 (26), CV3 30 (30), CV4 17 (18), CV5 30 (30).  A criterion that fails equally on the ideal control is a shot-count (or saturation) effect, not a noise effect.
- $r_\mathrm{nc}$ ranges over [0.996, 1.359]; it is within [0.996, 1.133] for the compressed families and [1.012, 1.359] for the frozen ones.  For the compressed families $f_\mathrm{hit}$ is a near-unbiased estimate of the near-clean fraction; for the frozen 2x3 circuits (model clean fraction about 0.03) reference hits also come from faulty trajectories that still land on the reference string, and $f_\mathrm{hit}$ overstates the clean fraction (the same effect the H0_diag hardware diagnostic of 2026-09-22 found, prompts/20).
- The percentile bootstrap of $E_R-E_0$ (shots resampled within each circuit) is shifted *up* relative to the point value: a resample repeats some shots and drops others, so it has fewer distinct states and a smaller $B_\mathrm{all}$.  Read its width as the shot-to-shot spread, not as an interval centred on the point value.
- **Extra noisy states can only lower $E_R$.**  Every noisy shot that decodes adds a valid basis state of the right sector, and the Ritz value on a larger basis is never higher (variational principle).  Noise also takes shots away from the clean distribution, which can miss important states and raise $E_R$; the two effects compete, and the noisy $E_R-E_0$ is below the ideal one in 12 of 18 rows with $E_R \ne E_0$.  A noisy $E_R-E_0$ below the ideal one is therefore not "better accuracy" but extra (noise-born) basis states; the quality measures are the certificate (is $E_0$ inside, how wide) and the recall of $S_{99.9}$.
- 2x2: the sectors (dimensions 38 and 20) saturate -- $B_\mathrm{all}$ is the whole sector with or without noise, so 2x2 is not a test of noise robustness; CV4 fails there because random bases saturate too (same label as `validation/CV_2x2_info.json`, "not a device test (saturation)").

## 5. Problems and assumptions

- Device models are Pauli channels built from published numbers only (report 002), placed at the level of the IR gate list (`traj_sparse.ir_events`); the frozen 2x3 circuits are simulated on their IR (2590 two-qubit gates), not on the compiled native circuits (fewer ZZ gates), as in report 003.
- The shot plan scales the noiseless plan of record by the *model* clean fraction $f_c$; on a real device $f_c$ would have to be measured first.
- Cuts (budget, section 4): 2x4 B=1 at the 1x D3'-R plan (section 3.3); Helios-1 2x4 classB_v2 at half the scaled shots; not sampled: Helios-1 frozen 2x3, Helios-1 2x4 classA_v2, 2x4 classA_v1 (their short noise runs and analytic clean fractions are in report 003).
- Bootstrap: B = 50 resamples at 2x4 (30-minute rule per analysis part), 200 elsewhere.
- The count of accepted states outside the ideal support is 0 everywhere because the coarse states have support on (almost) the whole sector; it does not separate noise-born states from clean ones.
- The wall-time factor for 10 threads in the budget was an assumption (budget note); the actual thread-hours are in section 2.
- The npz shot sequences (`<lattice>/noise/data/results/.../skqd_samples/`) are not committed (owner's call); the analysis JSONs are.
