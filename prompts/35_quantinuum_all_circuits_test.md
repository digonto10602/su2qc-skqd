### 3.3 T1 at 2x4 — gate Q0P_2x4 (laptop, background chunks; 6 h cap)

Build order: `--lattice 4 --k 1` (15 circuits + 2 calibration), then `--k 2`, `--k 3`, `--k 4`, each as its own
background command; stop adding $k$ values when the 6-h cap is reached and record `built_k` in the index.

**Q1L (local equivalence, every built circuit).**  Compile at level 0 and read the native circuit back with
`pytket_to_ir`.  The check: (i) the sequence of two-qubit gates (ordered pairs of qubits and angles, as `rzz`
in both) is identical to the IR's sequence after the prototype transpile, angle difference $< 10^{-12}$;
(ii) between consecutive two-qubit gates, for every qubit, the product of the one-qubit gates on that qubit
(a $2\times2$ unitary) equals the IR's product on the same qubit to $10^{-12}$ after global-phase removal;
(iii) the measurement map is $q_k \to c_k$, no implicit permutation.  Equal segment products with an identical
two-qubit skeleton imply equal unitaries, so Q1L is exact, not statistical.  If level 0 reorders or cancels
gates so that (i) fails, re-compile with the pure rebase (`AutoRebase({Rz, PhasedX, ZZPhase})`, no optimisation
pass; `compile_native(..., pure_rebase=True)`), freeze **that** circuit, and record `compile_mode` in the
manifest; Q0P_2x4 Q2 then compares the rebased ZZ count to `rzz_before` (equality expected).

**Q1S (statevector, as far as the laptop reaches).**  On the sparse path (`sparse_sim.run_sparse` on the
`pytket_to_ir` output, now with `rzz`) for `B0_ref0_k1` and `B1_ref0_k1` first — the IR coarse step took 191
and 191 s on this path (`S2_2x4.json data.circuits.circuits_4_exact.coarse_steps.B0_ref0_k1.sparse_wall_s`,
`circuits_4_exact_rest.coarse_steps.B1_ref0_k1.sparse_wall_s`), the native circuit has about 3-4x the gates
(planner arithmetic); cap 30 min per circuit.  Record per circuit `state_verified` (max abs(dpsi) vs
`krylov.coarse_states` on the codewords, leakage) or `sparse_capped_at_s`.  Then the remaining $k=1$ circuits,
then $k \ge 2$, inside the 6-h cap.
