## Diagnosis (three separate things, none of them a code bug)

**D1 — the width target is a 99.99 %-weight requirement, and the H1 width was read on the wrong one of the
manual's two certified intervals.**  The residual of the Ritz vector on $B$ is carried by the states outside $B$:
$r_H^2 = \sum_{n\notin B}\big|\sum_{b\in B}H_{nb}\psi_b\big|^2 \approx \sum_{n\notin B}(E_0 - H_{nn})^2|\Omega_n|^2$ when
$\psi_R \approx \Omega|_B$, i.e. $r_H \approx c\sqrt{1 - W(B)}$ with $c \sim 10$ at 2x3 (the diagonal energies of the
tail states sit 5-10 above $E_0$).  The prototype confirms it on the oracle supports (planner arithmetic reproduced
in `scratch/planner/oracle_width_20261005.json`, $B = 0$): $S_{99}$ (31 states) $r_H = 0.609$, estimate 0.753;
$S_{999}$ (86) $r_H = 0.265$, estimate 0.305; $S_{3\times10^{-4}}$ (116) 0.145; $S_{10^{-4}}$ (173) 0.100;
$S_{10^{-5}}$ (288) 0.033.  So **$r_H \le 0.1$ needs $W \ge 0.9999$: the 173-state support whose hardest state
has $\max_c p_c = 1.8\times10^{-5}$ in the signed family ($6\times10^6$ shots per sector by the $k = 4$-scaled bound
at $y = 0.0574$)** — unreachable at any D3'-R multiple, and unreachable by the very set ($S_{999}$) that H1's
recall target names.  The manual defines the certified interval $I_0 = [E_R - \delta, E_R]$ with $\delta = r_H$
(Weinstein) **or** $\delta = r_H^2/(\alpha - E_R)$ (Kato–Temple), Step 5.3, and its own worked example at
$f = 0.1$, $5\times10^4$ shots reports the Kato–Temple interval $[-5.622, -5.593]$ (width 0.029 at $r_H = 0.281$) as
the certified one.  On the Kato–Temple reading with the exact $E_1$ the oracle table gives width 0.139 at $S_{99}$
and **0.026 at $S_{999}$**: H1's two conditions (recall $\ge 0.8$ of $S_{999}$, width $\le 0.1$) are mutually
consistent only under that reading, and prompts/30 CV2 / CV3 applied the Weinstein width to H1 by my error.
Rigour: Kato–Temple with $\beta = E_1$ (the exact second level of the *model*, Table 1, a computed number that the
device does not supply) gives $E_0 \ge E_R - r_H^2/(E_1 - E_R)$ whenever $E_R < E_1$, with no further assumption
(`certify` already returns it as `kt_rigorous`); together with $E_R \ge E_0$ this is a rigorous two-sided
interval at 2x3.  The manual's "gap-assumed" label refers to $\alpha$ = second Ritz value (an upper bound on
$E_1$); at 2x3 the exact $E_1$ is available and must be used.  At $B = 1$ the cluster ($E_1 - E_0 = 0.024$) makes
the Kato–Temple width $r_H^2/0.022 \approx 0.5$-$0.9$ at the plan (the executor's `KT` column), so H2 stays what
the manual says: the cluster energy certified by Weinstein to $\pm r_H \le 0.15$.  On $B_{\rm all}$ the executor's
records give, at the $1\times$ plan: $B=0$ Kato–Temple width (exact $E_1$; planner arithmetic $r_H^2/(E_1 - E_R)$
on `data.curves`) at $N/2$: 0.020 / 0.025 / 0.020 ($f = 0.10$), 0.014-0.019 (0.05), 0.022-0.027 (0.15), at $N$
0.006-0.014 — all $\le 0.1$; $B=1$ Weinstein $r_H(N)$ = 0.137 / 0.125 / 0.143 ($f = 0.10$), 0.108-0.118 (0.05),
0.117-0.134 (0.15) — all $\le 0.15$ — but $r_H(N/2)$ = 0.18-0.21 in every $B=1$ cell: **H2's width is met at the
minimum plan with a 5-15 % margin and not at half of it.**

**D2 — the P9 $B_{\rm sig}$ rule is a saturation-regime device and the wrong basis for certificates at 2x3.**
P9 (prompts/24) keeps a state when its count is one-sided $3\sigma$ above $\mu_s = Na/\dim$.  At 2x2 ($Na/\dim$ =
16.97 / 15.72, `validation/H0_2x2.json`) every state receives garbage counts and $B_{\rm all} = \dim$: the cut is
what makes a device support exist.  At 2x3 $Na/\dim = 0.03$-$0.5$ over the ladder, so the cut keeps only states
seen 2 (at $1\times$) to 4-5 times (at $8\times$), and the states it drops are exactly the $S_{10^{-4}}\setminus S_{999}$
states that carry the residual: $W(B_{\rm sig})$ stalls at 0.9986-0.9990 at every $s$ while $W(B_{\rm all})$ reaches
0.9995-0.99965 (`data.resize_summary`, `data.curves`).  The cut also breaks nestedness (`B_sig non-nested steps
3`, CV0 information).  The manual's definition of the support is $B_{\rm all}$: "B is the union of accepted
configurations over all circuits, always including the references" (Step 5.1), with the false-positive count
reported (5.4); the certificates are rigorous for any $B$, so garbage states cannot invalidate them (they lower
$E_R$ towards $E_0$ and, in the records, lower $r_H$: $B_{\rm all}$ is below $B_{\rm sig}$ at every point).  Their
cost is confined to $|B|$: at $1\times$, $f = 0.10$, $B_{\rm all}$ has 185-208 states with 4-11 of weight $< 10^{-8}$
and an expected $\dim\,(1 - e^{-\mu_s}) \approx 52$ garbage-born states (planner arithmetic, $\mu_s = 0.077$), most of
which land on real tail states of tiny weight — a 25 % inflation of $|B|$ that makes the P1 comparison at
equal $|B|$ *conservative* for the device.  The P1 protocol of the manual is "top-$|B|$ by count" (Tables 3-4):
a ranked curve, on which the P9 cut is one point.

**D3 — CV3 tested the wrong step.**  $E_R(B^{(3)}) - E_R(B^{(4)}) > E_{\rm tol}$ says the $k = 4$ circuits reach
weight the $k \le 3$ circuits do not — which is why they are in the family (manual 4.2-4.3: the coarse family
with $k \le 4$ reaches 86 of 86 / 94 of 95 of $S_{999}$).  Convergence in $k$ is tested by the *next* step: does a
$k = 5$ coarse circuit still move the energy by more than $E_{\rm tol}$?  On the simulator a $k = 5$ circuit is
`coarse_states(groups, psi, dt, 5)[5]` — no device, no signature; a coarse circuit has the same gate count at
every $k$ (one step with angle $k\Delta t$; the $k$-independence of $f$ noted in prompts/27 S3H), so a family
extension would cost the same per shot.  The width does not belong in CV3 at all (it is CV2's object and, at
$B = 1$, shot-limited: `cv3_at_2Nc_information` shows $r_H(B^{(4)})$ on $B_{\rm all}$ falling from 0.17-0.19 at
$N_c$ to 0.11-0.14 at $2N_c$).
