## 1. Cause

### 1.1 STOP 2 (CV2 at B=1 beyond s = 8) is a shot limitation of the signed plan, not a defect of the family

The Weinstein half-width on a basis B is set by the ground-state weight B misses: on the executor's oracle table
r_H = 9.97, 9.90, 9.95 x sqrt(1 - W) at \|B\| = 1229, 1000, 1538 (*planner arithmetic* on `oracle_b1.json`), i.e.
r_H ~ 10 sqrt(1 - W) at 2x4 B=1 as at 2x3.  H2's r_H <= 0.15 therefore needs W >= 0.99977 of the B=1 ground state
inside B_all.  D3'-R sizes the plan for *recall of S999* (99.9 % of the weight) with probability 0.95 — and that is
exactly what the 1x plan delivers (recall 0.92, W 0.9987, r_H 0.25, E_R - E0 = 0.66 E_tol, section 0).  The gap
between 99.9 % and 99.98 % is the tail of the ground state beyond S999 (470 states); the oracle needs about 1550
states for it and the sampled support reaches the same W with about 1.3x more states (1538-1583 at 8N gives
r_H 0.121-0.131, the oracle gives 0.07 there).  That tail is reached only with shots: the signed re-sizing ladder
is the mechanism, and at 2x4 B=1 it runs past the grid's end.  The trend is regular (r_H(sN) 0.167, 0.144, 0.131 at
s = 4, 6, 8) and 2x3 B=1 showed the same mechanism one rung lower (s = 2 at every f, `validation/CV_2x3_plan.json
data.resize_summary`).  Two consequences the executor must verify:

- **Nested-prefix identity.**  The ladder reads the criteria at sN on the sN/2 prefix of the same samples, so
  r_H(16N/2) = r_H(8N) = 0.1305 max over seeds and r_H(12N/2) = r_H(6N) = 0.1437 max — both already <= 0.15 on the
  recorded samples.  The N clause of s = 12 (12N) and s = 16 (16N) needs samples beyond the cached 8N
  (section 3.1).  *Planner expectation*: s* = 12 (r_H(6N) 0.1437 passes by 4 %; r_H(12N) <= r_H(8N) = 0.1305 if the
  trend holds), s = 16 otherwise; the gate decides mechanically (smallest s).
- **The B=1 cluster is why the sampled support is less efficient than the oracle (planner hypothesis, to be
  tested by D1/D2 of section 3.5).**  The B=1 sector is a four-state cluster (E0..E3 within 0.152; E1 - E0 =
  0.00603).  A Krylov circuit applies one coarse step at angle k dt <= 4 x 0.1303 = 0.52 to a product reference,
  so the relative phases inside the cluster are at most 0.152 x 0.52 = 0.08 rad: no circuit of the signed family
  can separate psi_0 from psi_1..psi_3; every circuit samples the *cluster projection* of its reference plus the
  rotating higher states.  B_all therefore carries the tails of psi_1..psi_3 (states with no psi_0 weight, which
  do not lower r_H) and under-samples psi_0's own tail.  If this reading is right, D2 will show that B_all \ O
  (the sampled states outside the oracle set of equal size) carries much more psi_1..psi_3 weight than psi_0
  weight.  It also says why *more references* is the wrong lever: a fifth reference adds another cluster
  projection (the cluster subspace is 4-dimensional, 4 references already span it if D1's overlap matrix has rank
  4) and samples psi_0's tail at the rate \|<psi_0\|r>\|^2 \|psi_0(s)\|^2, smaller than the top references do.
  S99 coverage is not the problem (lambda_min(S99) 10.5 > 6.3, P(all S99 seen) 0.99993, `plan.json`).

### 1.2 STOP 1 (CV3 > E_tol at B=1) is the same shot limitation read on an allocation nobody runs

CV3 compares E_R(k <= 4) with E_R(k <= 5) at *equal* shots per circuit, N_eq = 1600 (25 600 shots).  At that
allocation E_R(k <= 4) - E0 = 0.0194-0.0246 = 1.5-2.0 E_tol (section 0) — twice the error of the D3'-R allocation
of the same total (0.0083-0.0086), because the equal split gives the floor circuits 1600 shots instead of 267
and the k = 4 circuits 1600 instead of 4400-6400, and D3'-R put the shots on k = 4 precisely because those
circuits carry the S999 tail.  Adding four k = 5 circuits at 1600 shots is 25 % more shots of the late-Krylov
kind, and it lowers E_R by 0.84-1.21 E_tol; doubling every circuit (CV1 at 1x) lowers it by 0.43-0.74 E_tol; at
2 N_eq the k = 5 step halves to 0.54-0.62 E_tol.  A family that is k-limited would show a k = 5 step insensitive
to shots; this one halves when the shots double.  Two facts fix the reading:

- **Variational bound.**  E_R(k <= 5) >= E0 on any basis, so 0 <= CV3 <= E_R(k <= 4; N_eq) - E0.  CV3 cannot fail
  once the k <= 4 equal-shots basis is itself within E_tol of E0.
- **Which plan.**  prompts/31 ruling 3 defines N_c as "the plan's sector total over the circuits"; prompts/31
  ruling 4 says the re-sized plan "replaces the D3'-R row of the plan table".  At 2x3 the gate read CV3 at the 1x
  total (where it passed, `cv3_at_2Nc_information` has 128 000 = 2 x 63 875 at B=0 f = 0.05) and the question
  which plan never arose.  At 2x4 B=1 it does: the plan that will be run is s* x D3'-R, and CV3's purpose
  (shot-limited or k-limited, prompts/30 section 3) is answered on that plan.  Reading CV3 at the final plan's
  N_eq is **owner item OD2** (an evaluation point of a criterion changes); the 1x reading, the 2 N_eq reading and
  the bound are recorded beside it in every case.  The STOP rule itself does not change: CV3 > E_tol at the
  signed reading point remains a STOP.  *Planner expectation* at N_eq(s* = 12) = 18 400 per circuit: the bound
  E_R(k <= 4; 18 400 x 16) - E0 is at the 1e-3 level (oracle at \|B\| ~ 1500: 4e-4; sampled ~1.3x less efficient),
  i.e. CV3 <= 0.1-0.2 E_tol; the gate computes it (section 3.3).
